# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Vietnamese English learners at beginner to lower-intermediate level, beginning with CEFR A2 vocabulary. The primary users are students and independent learners who want a simple, fast, low-pressure way to build practical everyday vocabulary in short or extended study sessions. The interface must remain understandable when the learner has limited English proficiency.

## Product Purpose

Provide an immediately accessible, public vocabulary-learning experience organized by CEFR level and everyday topic. Learners should be able to move from a topic into flashcards, multiple-choice practice, writing practice, or matching; classify words as learned or needing review; and return later with progress preserved locally. The first release succeeds when the complete A2 learning flow is fast, reliable, mobile-friendly, and easy to extend to later CEFR levels without a major rewrite.

## Brand Personality

Clear, calm, and encouraging. The product should feel modern and professionally made without feeling formal, childish, competitive, or high pressure. Vietnamese guidance should reduce uncertainty while English vocabulary remains the learning focus.

## Anti-references

- Do not clone ForumFlash branding, logo, source code, proprietary assets, exact visual styling, or copyrighted interface assets.
- Do not resemble a noisy gamified children’s app, a dense school administration tool, or a generic card-grid SaaS dashboard.
- Avoid excessive animation, decorative achievement mechanics, cluttered navigation, and English-only instructions that make beginner learners guess what to do.
- Do not let accessibility features add visible complexity when standard semantic and interaction patterns are sufficient.

## Design Principles

1. Begin studying without ceremony: no account, onboarding wall, or configuration step should stand between the learner and a useful session.
2. Keep one primary learning action obvious on every screen, with concise Vietnamese support where it reduces uncertainty.
3. Make progress trustworthy: learned, review, remaining, and session outcomes must reflect the loaded vocabulary and locally stored state.
4. Prefer calm, familiar interaction patterns over novelty so the interface disappears behind the learning task.
5. Design every flow for a narrow mobile screen and keyboard use from the start, not as later adaptations.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA where reasonably practical. Provide sufficient contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic HTML, accessible labels and names, keyboard-operable study modes, non-color-only feedback, comfortable mobile touch targets, and reduced-motion behavior. Keep the experience cognitively simple and ensure Vietnamese-speaking learners with limited English can understand navigation and instructions.
